# Proximate-Prime Quadratics: code and data

Code and data supporting two papers by Michael M. Ross (2026):

1. [*Proximate-Prime Quadratics: Forced Local Obstruction and a Conditioned Null Model*](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20694502) — **Paper 1**
2. [*Stacked Prime-Rich Quadratics and the Covering of Square Intervals*](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20788482) — **Paper 2**

Every number in both papers is regenerated by the scripts here. All results are
empirical or heuristic, confirmed by computation and not proven; in particular
the Bateman–Horn heuristic, the independence of witnesses, and the covering law
of Paper 2 are used as heuristics throughout.

## The objects

A **proximate-prime polynomial (PPP)** is the quadratic $f(n)=an^2+bn+c$ through
four consecutive primes $p_0<p_1<p_2<p_3$ at $n=1,2,3,4$. It exists exactly when
the gaps $g_1,g_2,g_3$ form an arithmetic progression, and then

$$2a=g_2-g_1,\qquad b=g_1-3a,\qquad c=p_0-a-b,\qquad D=b^2-4ac=(g_1-a)^2-4ap_0 .$$

The **Bateman–Horn constant** is used throughout in the single-polynomial
normalization

$$C_f=\prod_p\frac{1-N_p(f)/p}{1-1/p},$$

with $N_p$ the number of roots of $f$ modulo $p$. The expected number of primes
among $f(1),\ldots,f(N)$ is $\hat\pi_f(N)=\sum_{n\le N}C_f/\log f(n)$. For the
always-odd forms this equals $2\,C(\Delta)$ in the notation of Jacobson–Williams;
Euler's polynomial has $C_f=6.64$. The **nonresidue score** is
$S(D)=\#\{p\in\{5,7,\ldots,31\}:(D/p)=-1\}$.

## What the papers find

**Paper 1.** Four prime values at $n=1,\ldots,4$ forbid roots in four residue
classes modulo every prime. That forces $N_2=N_3=0$ and $N_5\le1$ for every PPP
and pushes the discriminant toward quadratic nonresidues. A random quadratic
conditioned on this, in the observed gap patterns, reproduces the distribution
of $S(D)$ across the family at every range from $10^6$ to $10^{10}$;
consecutiveness contributes a stable residual of $-0.03$ in mean $S$. The prime
density is the Bateman–Horn prediction to $1.001\pm0.026$ per curve. The same
conditioning predicts, with no free parameter, an index-dependent deficit in the
yield just beyond the interpolated values. A scan of the constant term shows the
construction catches only about $2(C_f/\log c)^4$ of the curves with a given
constant and misses the best curves in every range.

**Paper 2.** If the witnesses of different curves in a square interval
$B_k=[k^2,(k+1)^2)$ were independent, the number of curves needed to cover every
band up to $M$ would be $K(M)\approx2(\log M)^2/\overline{C}$. This holds for a
stack of high-constant curves with spread constants ($K$ observed $=$ independent
surrogate within noise to $M=10^5$). The dependence between two curves in a band
is the pair singular series of $f_i(n)f_j(n+t)$ at the landing offset
$t\approx(c_i-c_j)/2k$; it averages to exactly $1$ over offsets, so constants
spread over many band-widths stack independently while constants within a
band-width freeze the pair constants. The formula predicts per-pair witness
correlations with correlation $0.90$–$0.96$ and no fitted parameter, and it
contains the two effects reported in the first version: the eligibility
partition of half-even curves $n^2+c$ is its $p=2$ case and the residual
correlation of low-$C_f$ stacks its odd-prime case.

The CSVs and summaries for $10^6$ to $10^9$ are not stored because they
regenerate in seconds to a minute (below). The $10^{10}$ CSV is 92 MB and is not
stored either; its summary is.

## Paper 1: `ppp_gen.py`

A single script. It builds a segmented, bit-packed sieve of Eratosthenes to a
bound covering every polynomial value counted, detects arithmetic-progression
gap triples on the fly, and computes for each record the coefficients,
discriminant, Legendre signature, root counts, shift-alias flag, $C_f$ over
$p\le997$, the Bateman–Horn prediction $\hat\pi_f$, and the observed prime
count. It then evaluates the null models and writes a CSV and a text summary.

```
python3 ppp_gen.py 1e6                          # the 10^6 family: ppp1e06.csv + ppp1e06_summary.txt
python3 ppp_gen.py 1e8 --out ppp1e8             # 10^8, 9 s
python3 ppp_gen.py 1e10 --out ppp1e10           # 10^10, 750 s, 630 MB sieve
python3 ppp_gen.py 1e6 --index-start 0          # reproduce the n = 0..999 counts of the original data file
python3 ppp_gen.py 1e8 --sampled-null 100       # add the sampled non-consecutive null
python3 ppp_gen.py 1e6 --catalog "1,1;2,0;2,2"  # catalog scan (Paper 1 §6), one CSV per form
python3 ppp_gen.py 1e10 --no-counts             # scores and nulls only (fast)
```

| option | default | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `N` | — | upper bound for $p_0$, e.g. `1e6` or `100000000` |
| `--terms T` | 1000 | number of values $f(n)$ counted |
| `--index-start s` | 1 | first index counted; `0` reproduces the original file's convention |
| `--P` | 997 | cutoff of the Euler product for $C_f$ |
| `--sp` | `5,…,31` | primes of the Legendre signature |
| `--keep-negative` | off | keep decreasing-gap quadruples ($a<0$) |
| `--sampled-null K` | 0 | realize the gap-matched null with up to $K$ random primes per $(a,g_1)$ pattern |
| `--no-counts` | off | skip prime counting and $C_f$ |
| `--catalog FORMS` | — | scan every odd $c<N$ for the forms `a,b;a,b;…`, rank by $C_f$, report the PPP catch rate |
| `--catalog-top` | 100 | curves kept per form |
| `--seg`, `--out`, `--seed` | | sieve segment size, output prefix, RNG seed |

**Runtime and memory** (single core):

| $N$ | records / distinct | wall time | peak RSS |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10^6$ | 2,219 / 1,973 | 1 s | small |
| $10^8$ | 115,529 / 106,433 | 9 s | 180 MB |
| $10^9$ | 895,335 / 833,923 | 46 s | 430 MB |
| $10^{10}$ | 7,126,670 / 6,693,752 | 750 s | 630 MB sieve |

The sieve was validated against $\pi(x)$ at $10^6$, $10^7$, $10^8$,
$4\times10^8$, and against `sympy.isprime` on 20,000 random values.

**CSV columns.** `p0,p1,p2,p3,a,b,c,D,g1,S_leg,S_root,N2,N3,N5,distinct,primes,pct,C_f,bh_pred`.
`S_leg` is the paper's $S(D)$ by Legendre symbol; `S_root` counts primes with
$N_p=0$ (they differ only when $p\mid a$). `distinct` flags the first record of
each shift-orbit, keyed by $(a,\,b \bmod 2a,\,D)$. Catalog CSVs have
`c,D,C_f,primes,is_ppp`.

**Summary file.** Records and distinct curves, the distribution of $a$, the
forced facts $N_2=N_3=0$, $N_5\le1$, the $S(D)$ histogram over records and
distinct curves, the uniform and gap-matched null models (mean $S$, $P(S=9)$,
expected count of $S=9$ curves), the Bateman–Horn check with and without the
four constructed values, correlations, the threshold table with mean $C_f$ per
class, the top-100 / middle-100 / bottom-100 groups, and the top ten curves by
$C_f$ against the asymptotic ERH ceiling $2e^\gamma\log\log|D|$.

**Conventions.** Only $a>0$ is kept unless `--keep-negative`. Counts run over
$n=1,\ldots,1000$. The original data file
[`PPP1000000.txt`](https://naturalnumbers.org/PPP1000000.txt), the source of
the first version's statistics, counts $n=0,\ldots,999$ and contains two
duplicated rows (2,221 records against 2,219 here); `--index-start 0`
reproduces its counts.

### Results reproduced (Paper 1)

Mean $S(D)$ of distinct curves against the gap-matched null (the uniform null
gives $5.849$ at every range), and the Bateman–Horn ratio with the four
constructed values excluded:

| $N$ | $S$ observed | $S$ gap-matched null | difference | $S=9$ obs / expected | $\pi_f/\hat\pi_f$, $n\ge5$ | record $C_f$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $10^6$ | $5.924\pm0.031$ | 5.993 | −0.069 | 40 / 46 | 0.9936 | 7.95 |
| $10^7$ | $5.907\pm0.012$ | 5.942 | −0.035 | 280 / 307 | 0.9949 | 7.95 |
| $10^8$ | $5.863\pm0.004$ | 5.899 | −0.036 | 1,949 / 2,195 | 0.9953 | 8.25 |
| $10^9$ | $5.824\pm0.002$ | 5.860 | −0.036 | 14,735 / 16,286 | 0.9955 | 8.79 |
| $10^{10}$ | $5.797\pm0.001$ | 5.827 | −0.030 | 115,369 / 124,648 | 0.9957 | 9.13 |

With all 1,000 values the ratio at $10^6$ is $1.0011\pm0.0259$ (the surplus is
the four primes by construction); 20,000 random quadratics give
$1.0000\pm0.0004$. Catch rate of the construction among the hundred best curves
of a form (Paper 1 Table 5): 16, 10, 5 of 100 for $n^2+n+c$, $2n^2+c$,
$2n^2+2n+c$ below $10^6$; 3 and 4 of 100 for the first two forms below
$10^{10}$. None of the best curves in any range is a PPP.

### Figure pipeline (Paper 1)

`data/analysis.json` holds the diagnostics behind Figures 1 and 2: the observed
and expected $S(D)$ histograms at $10^6$, the per-class root-count means, the
threshold table, and the per-index Bateman–Horn ratio at $10^8$. It is produced
by `analysis.py` (diagnostics) and `profile_pred.py` (the exact per-index
prediction of the gap-matched null, computed per gap pattern), both of which
import `ppp_gen.py`. The figure coordinate lists in the paper's TikZ source are
taken from this file.

## Paper 2

### `stack_exp.py`

Two experiments, both on always-odd curves $n^2+n+c$, using the sieve of
`ppp_gen.py` extended to $10^{10}$ (630 MB, about 35 s). Run it in `code/`:

```
python3 stack_exp.py
```

**A — covering law (Paper 2 §2).** The hundred best $n^2+n+c$ with odd
$c<10^4$ ($\overline C=5.24$), witnesses in every band from $k_0=100$ to
$10^5$; $K(M)$ measured as the least covering prefix of a random ordering,
averaged over 200 orderings; compared with an independent surrogate (coins with
the Bateman–Horn probabilities), with $2(\log M)^2/\overline C$, and with the
second-order form of the paper. Also prints the detrended per-band
over-dispersion in three windows.

**B — pair correlations (Paper 2 §3).** Forty curves with constants spread over
$[10001,29463]$, all classes modulo 3; the witness correlation $r_{ij}$ of all
780 pairs in three windows ($k\approx2000$–$5000$, $2\times10^4$,
$6\times10^4$) against the pair singular series evaluated at the landing
offsets actually occurring, roots modulo $p\le97$. Prints the pool means, the
means by residue class of the constants modulo 3, the per-pair correlation, and
the over-dispersion; writes `stack_B.json` with the per-pair values.

Selected output (full values in Paper 2 Tables 1–2):

| $M$ | $K$ observed | independent surrogate | $2(\log M)^2/\overline C$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10^3$ | $14.5\pm2.5$ | $14.2\pm2.4$ | 18.2 |
| $10^4$ | $26.5\pm3.4$ | $27.0\pm3.6$ | 32.4 |
| $10^5$ | $42.8\pm4.5$ | $46.0\pm6.2$ | 50.6 |

| window | mean offset | pool-mean $r_{ij}$ obs / pred | per-pair correlation | over-dispersion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $k\approx2000$–$5000$ | 1.05 | −0.028 / −0.031 | 0.90 | 0.90 |
| $k\approx2\times10^4$ | 0.49 | −0.030 / −0.034 | 0.94 | 0.86 |
| $k\approx6\times10^4$ | 0.00 | −0.006 / −0.012 | 0.96 | 0.85 |

In the frozen window the pairs with $c_i\equiv c_j\equiv0\pmod3$ have
$r_{ij}=+1.85$ (predicted $+1.83$) and the pairs with $c_i\equiv0$,
$c_j\equiv1$ have $-1.00$ (predicted $-1.00$).

### `resid_pred.py`

Rebuilds the three `cf_resolve` pools of `ppp_repro.py` exactly (the first 55,
55, 45 odd constants above $10^6$ with $C_f$ in each band), recomputes the
pairwise witness excess in the same window, and predicts it from the pair
singular series at offset 0 with roots modulo $p\le997$. About 45 s, `sympy`
only.

| $C_f$ band | constants mod 3 (0,1,2) | measured | predicted | per-pair correlation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.6–3.4 | (0, 18, 37) | $+0.045\pm0.006$ | +0.049 | 0.88 |
| 3.4–4.3 | (0, 9, 46) | $+0.017\pm0.004$ | +0.020 | 0.81 |
| 4.3–7.0 | (0, 1, 44) | $-0.010\pm0.002$ | −0.005 | 0.58 |

The measured column reproduces the first version's table to the last digit.

### `ppp_repro.py`

The seven experiments of the first version of Paper 2, kept because the
revised paper quotes their numbers. `oddpool.json` must be in the working
directory. Run one target or all:

```
python3 ppp_repro.py law          # or: tworegime | strict | roughness | multiscale | cf_resolve | factor_pin
python3 ppp_repro.py all | tee ppp_repro_log.txt     # about 155 s
```

| target | revised paper | reproduces |
|---|---|---|
| `law` | §2 | 75-curve window test: $\overline\rho=0.1597$ vs $0.1588$, $49.6\pm5.2$ curves to cover 2,989 bands vs $50.1$ |
| `multiscale` | §5 | over-dispersion and hard-band tail of the fixed 40-curve pool from value $2.4\times10^7$ to $10^{12}$ |
| `tworegime` | §4 | clustered vs spread half-even pools: eligibility over-dispersion 60 vs 0.03, the 50% ceiling and its recovery |
| `factor_pin` | §4 | the half-even factor is 2: density ratio 1.94, covering ratio 1.92 (10%) and 1.98 (5%) |
| `cf_resolve` | §5 | the low-$C_f$ residual: $+0.045$, $+0.017$, $-0.010$ by $C_f$ band |
| `roughness` | §6 | $P_1/P_2/P_3^+$ split over $n=1,\ldots,14000$ for the first $c\ge41$ with $C_f>4.5$, $2.3<C_f<2.6$, $1.2<C_f<1.45$ |
| `strict` | §6 | four consecutive semiprimes on a quadratic: 5.87% of positions, shuffled-gap ratio 1.007, $C_f\approx2.74$ |

Two properties of this script matter for interpretation. Its pool builders take
the first constants above $10^6$ with $C_f$ in a stated band, in order, so every
pool is clustered within a few thousand and sits in the frozen regime of Paper 2
§3 at every window used. And its `multiscale` over-dispersion is relative to a
binomial with the pooled mean, which heterogeneous marginals push below 1; the
`excessVar` column is the matched-surrogate quantity. Its $C_f$ is truncated at
$p<2000$ (hence $6.65$ for Euler's polynomial), against $p\le997$ in
`ppp_gen.py`; the difference is a per-curve fluctuation of order 1% and does not
affect any comparison.

## Caveats

- Everything is heuristic: Bateman–Horn is assumed, independence is measured
  not proved, and the covering law follows from independence.
- $C_f$ is a truncated Euler product; do not quote it to more than three figures.
- Seeds are fixed, so runs are deterministic; the covering counts of experiment
  A and of `law` carry standard deviations over orderings.
- `oddpool.json` is a specific 40-curve pool; the multi-scale conclusion does
  not depend on it, the exact numbers do.
- The sieve bound in `stack_exp.py` is $10^{10}$; it needs about 700 MB.
